# Design intent and journey

PATCHSMITH uses a quiet cream/charcoal foundation and the approved Sunset interlock as its expressive identity. Original illustrative specimens make the five patch materials tangible. Large type, deliberate spacing, and changes of surface provide the visual rhythm.

The home journey is:
1. A clear brand proposition and two paths: explore materials or start a brief.
2. An editorial Pakistan-craftsmanship story, with Bilal Maqsood named subtly and the user-requested sweatshirt concepts shown prominently.
3. A five-material studio with manually selectable material states and an optional comparison table.
4. Application-specific guidance, carrying material and use-case preferences into the brief.
5. An explicit explanation of specification, approval and production conversations.
6. A guided four-step brief with a reviewable summary and honest local download.

The three user-provided references informed the scale of typography, editorial chapter changes and deliberate interaction states. PATCHSMITH does not copy their assets, proprietary typefaces, code, logo systems, motion sequences or commercial claims. There is no intro gate, scroll-jacking, mandatory animation, carousel autoplay or hover-only content.

The garment designs and patch specimens are explicitly conceptual. Neither is evidence of a manufactured sample. The brief is a prototype interaction rather than a quote, transaction or live sales submission. This design intentionally leaves commercial facts, production guarantees and customer evidence unfilled until approved.

## Thread-to-stitch transitions

A fine Sunset thread follows normal page scrolling into the Pakistan story and the craft-process section, then becomes small stitches. It is decorative, not a progress requirement. All content remains visible without JavaScript; reduced motion presents the complete divider. Desktop and mobile have their own SVG geometry. The portable Webflow version is provided as separate markup, CSS and footer JavaScript; native Webflow installation and testing are a separate step.
